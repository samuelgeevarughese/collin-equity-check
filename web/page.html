How the comparison works

  1. Candidates are homes in the same Collin CAD appraisal neighborhood and construction class, with living area within 15% and year built within 8 years. If fewer than 5 qualify, the window widens to 25% and 15 years.
  2. The 10 closest matches by size, age and pool are kept. Homes with fewer than 3 matches get no estimate.
  3. Each comparable is adjusted to your home: its improvement value per square foot is applied to your living area, plus your own land value.
  4. The equity value is the median of those adjusted values. Everything uses 2026 notice (pre-protest) values from the appraisal district.

Limits: this covers the equity argument only. Texas does not publish sale prices, so it cannot test whether your value is above market. Condition, updates, lot premiums and views are not in the public data, so treat the result as a starting point for your evidence, not a verdict. Not legal or tax advice.

Where are appraisals least consistent?

Comparables come from inside each neighborhood, so this can't say a whole neighborhood is over-appraised. What it can show is how uneven the appraisals are: the share of homes valued 5% or more above their own comparables. The higher that share, the more owners there have a solid equity case.
